# Assessment Governance and Professional Oversight

**Status:** Must be approved by the designated assessment professional before real use  
**Version:** 0.1

## 1. Purpose
Define how the platform registers, creates, reviews, publishes and retires questionnaires and assessment instruments. This document does not create a psychological test, scoring rule or clinical protocol.

## 2. Assessment categories
1. Locally developed school questionnaire: created by an authorized school user for a defined educational purpose; not represented as validated.
2. Classroom observation: structured description of observable behaviour in a specific context.
3. Personality/strengths instrument: only used when suitability, validation, licensing, age range, language and interpretation have been reviewed.
4. Emotional-wellbeing screening: separately governed, restricted to approved instruments and appropriately qualified professionals.

## 3. Required instrument registry fields
- Instrument name and exact version
- Owner/publisher and license terms
- Intended construct and purpose
- Intended age range and population
- Supported language(s)
- Validation evidence and limitations
- Administration instructions and accessibility considerations
- Scoring method source and version, if licensed/approved
- Qualification requirements
- Approval status, approver and date
- Review date and retirement criteria
- Permitted audience and reporting language

## 4. Lifecycle
Draft → content review → assessment/psychometric review as applicable → privacy/access review → approval → publication → periodic review → retirement.

A published version must not be modified in place. Create a new version and preserve the version used for every assignment/result.

## 5. School-created questionnaires
- Must state owner, purpose and intended audience.
- Must use neutral, non-stigmatizing wording.
- Must not ask teachers to diagnose disorders.
- Must be labelled “school-created questionnaire” or equivalent.
- Must not generate a clinical or validated psychometric score unless separately supported and approved.
- Must include review and retirement controls.
- Free-text prompts must warn against unnecessary sensitive information.

## 6. Observations
Record:
- date/time and context;
- observable action rather than inferred trait;
- frequency/duration only when meaningful and reliably recorded;
- relevant setting and support already tried;
- reviewer and follow-up status.

Do not convert “quiet”, “angry”, “prefers solitude” or similar descriptors into disease or fixed personality labels. Behaviour can be context-dependent.

## 7. Scoring and reporting
- Implement only documented, approved scoring rules.
- Keep scoring version and source traceable.
- Do not invent cut-offs, norms or clinical thresholds.
- Show limitations and uncertainty.
- Reports must distinguish raw responses, calculated results, observations and professional interpretation.
- Do not make high-stakes educational decisions solely from an automated result.

## 8. Professional screening
- Use only instruments approved for the intended context and age group.
- Verify qualifications, scope, assignment and required consent.
- Restrict raw answers, results and notes.
- Define referral, escalation, follow-up and record retention with the professional owner.
- Do not launch the module until the responsible professional and legal reviewer sign off.

## 9. Quality monitoring
Review user complaints, item ambiguity, missing data, language suitability, adverse effects and misuse. Any change to instrument content or scoring requires versioning and review. Do not infer validity from usage volume or user satisfaction.
